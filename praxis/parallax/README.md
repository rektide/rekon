# Parallax

**Look at the same emerging system from deliberately different positions.** The
differences can expose relationships, hidden assumptions, and missing dimensions
that no single account makes obvious. The goal is not more opinions or an average
answer; it is a better-grounded architectural understanding and a useful next move.

This is human-requested draft praxis, not a controlled scientific result, installed
skill, or requirement to use several agents for every task. It extends
[candidates](/praxis/candidates/README.md): candidates develop alternatives;
parallax also develops different views of one candidate or shared situation.

## Two useful modes

| Mode | What varies | What the comparison can reveal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Independent views of the same question** | Author/model, research path, interpretation; shared starting brief | Missed sources, conflicting factual claims, different uncertainty handling, and contrasting architectural insight |
| **Complementary design axes** | A named concern such as domain reduction, recording/recovery, or schedule identity | How one concern constrains another; which shared concepts genuinely align, and where integration would erase important differences |

These modes can coexist. Two models can investigate one source question while
several focused designers explore different axes. Do not call complementary
assignments a model benchmark, or call differently primed authors independent
replications. Record what each actually saw and was asked to do.

# Commission views that can teach each other later

Start with the human's useful experience and the system's existing evidence.
Choose a few consequential viewpoints rather than asking everyone to redesign
the whole destination. A good axis has its own concrete caller and failure cases,
and something important to say to another axis.

For each assignment, state:

- The question, scope, owned artifact, and intended reader.
- Shared constraints and which assumptions may be challenged.
- Source homes/revisions and prior material the author may use.
- What parallel work the author **must not read yet**.
- Examples or evidence sufficient to make the view testable or assessable.
- The checkpoint that will permit comparison and dependent work.

Avoid scripting the conclusion into the brief. Equally, do not remove all context
in pursuit of independence: authors need the same genuine user intent and source
constraints, not a guessing contest. A model-specific prompt can accommodate tool
or context limits, but differences belong in the comparison's provenance.

## Preserve acquisition before cooperation

An independent author writes and commits its own model-marked inquiry before
reading sibling outputs. Shared briefs are allowed; unpublished partner findings
are not. Authors can finish at different times. A gate delays **dependent reading
or synthesis**, not their ability to commit a coherent stage.

After the declared gate, cooperation becomes useful: exchange source corrections,
test a counterexample, revise an interface, and compare new possibilities. Name
the transition from independent to collaborative work. Do not pretend the revised
draft remains an untouched independent result.

Shared maintained documents need a different discipline: assign one write slot
at a time, or merge clearly owned additions deliberately. A consumer note or root
architecture document can otherwise leak one author's conclusions into another's
still-independent investigation. State which revision a subsequent author may read.

# Use evidence gates without freezing all progress

Wait when an in-flight result could materially change the contract being designed.
Continue on independent work when it cannot. This is not “wait for every agent” or
“never wait”; it is explicit dependency reasoning.

For example, a reducer-only interface can be explored against verified upstream
assessment types while a journal durability question remains behind an explicit
port. But selecting the implementation of a recording barrier must wait for that
journal evidence. Do not smuggle an unresolved assumption into a helper's name and
call the dependency satisfied.

Research, design-assessment, and follow-up comparison consume the same declared
concurrency budget. Renaming a research task “assessment” does not create a free
slot. Prefer a small number of genuinely independent topics over many superficially
parallel pieces whose outputs cannot yet be used.

## Rate limits and partial evidence

If one author is blocked by provider capacity, record the missing result and its
effect on gates. Another author may continue independently; infrastructure failure
is not evidence of lower research quality. Do not fill the gap by reading the
available answer into the blocked author's future prompt and still claim a fair
independent comparison.

The parent can record provisional learning with its provenance. The human may
authorize proceeding without the missing view, but that changes the comparison
conditions; it does not retroactively supply evidence. Preserve the queued task,
source inputs, and forbidden-reading instruction for resumption.

# Parent synthesis is architectural work

The parent remains responsible for the system's shape. Read enough of the actual
source and proposed interfaces to judge them; passing checks and polished summaries
do not outsource that responsibility. A synthesis should explain:

1. **Common ground:** claims independently supported, and whether agreement comes
   from distinct evidence or simply the same supplied framing.
2. **Useful differences:** what each position makes visible, including non-competing
   alternatives and capabilities worth keeping open.
3. **Tensions:** conflicting facts, incompatible lifetimes/guarantees, or interfaces
   that fit one caller but harm another. Resolve through evidence or preserve the
   unresolved question explicitly.
4. **Selection and delta:** what to retain, combine, split, postpone, or test next;
   what the chosen synthesis loses as well as gains.
5. **Next coherent action:** a bounded proof, focused revision, or implementation
   slice with the necessary inputs now available.

Synthesis commonly follows parallax; it is not automatically a better design
because it is newer or includes a little of every source. Sometimes the right
result is one selected design, separate designs for different callers, or a sharper
question. A source-cited overall architecture should come **after** core contracts
are usefully sketched, not predetermine them and solicit decorative agreement.

## Compare outputs, not model reputations

When the human wants to learn about models, compare these particular outputs on
the work actually assigned:

| Dimension | Useful evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Source fidelity | Correct APIs, versions, code paths, and distinction between code/docs/proposals |
| Architectural insight | A real simplification, preserved distinction, or new useful path |
| Uncertainty calibration | Honest limits without either invented guarantees or blanket paralysis |
| Caller leverage | Clear independent uses, locality, and composition that removes real complexity |
| Communication economy | Enough motivation and evidence to act, without drowning the seam in caveats |
| Revision behavior | Concrete response to counterexamples or source corrections |

A quick result may be valuable without being exhaustive; an exhaustive result
may still miss the organizing idea. Prompt, tools, source access, context, and
execution interruptions confound broad model rankings. Report characteristic
strengths/weaknesses observed **here**, not universal claims about an entire family.

# Motivating case: Cyclrs and Kubeless

Cyclrs began with README-driven contracts for quota-period observation, journal
history, systemd scheduling, and independently assembled Rust microapps. Kubeless
then changed substantially. The operator asked the parent to reground the consumer
in current upstream, compare FX/Luna research, commission several Astra-medium
design axes, and later use GX for small related README/architecture bundles.

The initial source audit found that explicit non-advancing observation assessment
and finite native-context passes now existed upstream, changing an older
SemanticGate-centered discussion. That is a concrete example of a changed source
expanding the design; it is not proof that the eventual integration works.

The emerging axes are domain reducer/reconciler composition, recording/recovery,
and schedule identity/fencing. The parent will integrate them after meaningful
checkpoints. A separate
[Kubeless consumer note](file:///home/rektide/src/kubeless/doc/consumer/cyclrs.md)
returns motivation, verified fit, and open needs upstream without making Kubeless
the owner of Cyclrs policy or claiming a dependency that has not been implemented.

This case is **in progress**. FX resumed after a capacity limit; both paired source
studies and focused corrections are now available. The
[journal comparison](file:///home/rektide/src/cyclrs/design/architecture-refresh/journal-syn0.gpt6astra.md)
found an important low-code alternative in FX's account—stock telemetry plus
required exact read-back—while Luna better separated synchronization and persistence
guarantees. The [timer comparison](file:///home/rektide/src/cyclrs/design/architecture-refresh/timer-syn0.gpt6astra.md)
found useful FX implementation detail but stronger initial guarantee calibration
in Luna; even a correction mistakenly turned one creation call into atomic setup.
These judgments are scoped to the actual reports, not model-family rankings.

Three complementary design axes are committed, and a fresh independent design
assessment pair is reviewing their joins. The parent integration draft is withheld
from that pair: cross-review of the candidates is intentional, ratification of the
parent's preferences is not. No compiled integration or promoted crate map is
claimed. The case's source briefs, outputs, and eventual synthesis stay
[Cyclrs-side](file:///home/rektide/src/cyclrs/design/architecture-refresh/README.md).
This praxis records the method and its current motivation, not a second product
roadmap or an invented success story.

## Lessons from the still-running case

- **A correction is not proof.** Check whether a repaired sentence imports a new
  unsupported guarantee. “One request” and “transactional setup” can still differ.
- **Use counterexamples, not bigger taxonomies.** An older valid snapshot with a
  missing newer tail exposed an evidence limit more clearly than adding another
  generic failure state. The human's “systemd is the supervisor” direction then
  simplified recovery ownership without changing that factual limit.
- **Keep original and revised evidence distinguishable.** A source review that
  supplied the missing insight is not evidence that the original author discovered
  it independently. Compare initial calibration and revision behavior separately.
- **Recovery must preserve authorship and inputs.** Restart/duplicate-dispatch
  artifacts produced different texts at the same path. Commit both rather than
  discard one; designate exact assessment inputs; use unique output paths; verify
  file contents instead of assuming a final summary describes the current bytes.
  Cancellation acknowledgement alone did not prevent a previously running task
  from later producing output in this case. This is an orchestration lesson, not
  an architectural advantage earned by adding agents.

# How to tell whether parallax helped

Did another position expose a consequential omission? Did verified upstream
capability change the design? Can callers use the selected interfaces more clearly?
Did the parent preserve distinctions that a premature synthesis would have erased?
Can we name a next action and the evidence that permits it?

More agents, more documents, unanimity, or a completed comparison table are not
the result. Record useful surprises and failures through
[case stories](/evidence/README.md#evidence-case-stories) when they teach something.
