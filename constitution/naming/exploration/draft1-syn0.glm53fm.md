## Where The Wave Converged

1. **The corpus is citatorily isolated, and everyone measured it.** Zero
   external markdown links point into the naming tree; the only
   controlled external references are one or two beads records (counts
   differ by snapshot). Five independent inventories — anchors 398, 398,
   401, 411, 416 — disagree only because files were landing while each
   was counted, which surveyor turned into a method point: seal the
   inbound inventory before any move.
2. **The move is cheap now and will not get cheaper.** Tally's
   act-at-acceptance finding and assay's `naming-escape-window` are the
   same monotone-cost observation at two grains: one a dated
   measurement, the other a general pricing law. They compose rather
   than compete.
3. **Frontmatter identity fields are projections, and they drift.** All
   five apply the companion's "compatibility or indexing projections"
   ruling to the corpus's own `resource` / `local_knowledge_id` /
   `qualified_knowledge_id` fields; assessor supplies the mechanical
   form: `resource` equals own path or is absent.
4. **The bundle root is undeclared, and four of five demand it.** Tally
   makes the authority declaration an identity prerequisite; assessor
   mints `naming-bundle-root`; surveyor mints `naming-bundle-boundary`
   and separates it from hierarchy1's docroot; luna sequences it before
   any cross-tree reference. Two of those are one concept minted twice
   within the same wave — see [the wave's own collision](#the-waves-own-collision).
5. **Section grain dominates, and it is ours.** Roughly 96 percent of
   the tree's identities are section-grain; OKF settles only the
   document shell. Every document says some version of: the extension
   must be declared as an extension, and anchor verification is
   identity-critical, not style.
6. **The tool seam's backlog is now concrete.** Anchor checking,
   fragment-through-symlink behavior, self-reference hygiene, flattened
   projection rules, hat citation form, a linter-grade anchor/heading
   similarity check — every document leaves work there, and the lists
   overlap heavily.

## What Still Diverges

- **Stub semantics.** Surveyor's tombstone law says a regular markdown
  stub at an old path is an identity-bearing deprecated Concept under
  OKF; the loose "old path is only a retired resolution form" phrasing
  in the other responses is, per surveyor, not literally true of an
  OKF-conformant file. Luna independently converges (stub = deprecated
  redirecting document); tally and assessor keep the symlink-first
  reading. This is a real, resolvable disagreement about what a stub
  *is*.
- **Symlink exposure.** Tally and assessor treat a directory symlink as
  the cheap compatibility default; surveyor demotes it to a verified
  local optimization until hosts promise fragment behavior.
- **Anchor length.** Tally alone demotes the constitution's
  anchor-prefix rule to readability counsel; nobody contradicts it, but
  nobody adopts it either. It is the wave's most exposed new claim.
- **Actor minting.** Four mint; luna declines with the argument that
  precedent is permission, not obligation. The wave did not resolve
  whether a synthesis-class document owes an accountable voice.
- **Audit fidelity.** Assay alone challenges an ask-audit row (the
  model-qualified resolution ask should read *survives, inverted*). The
  audit has not yet recorded the amendment.
