# Adversarial Review Of Naming Draft 2

The reviewed draft has a strong architectural center: native systems retain
their own identities, current references are distinguished from exact evidence
and mutable channels, moves are represented as changes rather than erased, and
derived indexes do not become naming authorities. Its correction of the earlier
Pandoc anchor verification is also factually valuable.

It is not ready for acceptance. The central name grammar differs from both the
recorded human ruling and OKF while being described as an adoption. The proposed
repository-root namespace has no membership rule. Its cross-project authority
component is only a locally ambiguous alias. It also narrows or omits surviving
human asks concerning actor names, durable ideas, alternate actor names, and
reference forms. Finally, the rename covenant weakens the ruling's human gate,
and the acceptance contract performs constitutional implementation that the
ticket assigns to a separate plan.

The smallest sound revision is not a wholesale rewrite. It is to settle the
identity spelling explicitly, define namespace admission and authority
qualification, restore or openly revise the missing asks, make rename governance
normative, and remove claims that the available measurements do not establish.

## Overall Assessment

The draft succeeds as a broad synthesis of migration, evidence, native-plane,
and proportional-governance concerns. In particular, its current/exact/channel
distinction at `draft2.sol56x.md`, lines 328-339, its change table at lines
423-436, and its warning against a second authority at lines 193-195 are useful
building blocks.

The draft is weaker as a constitution. Several decisions are expressed through
qualified prose rather than rules whose applicability can be determined. The
most important tuple cannot identify which repository paths participate, its
authority value cannot disambiguate projects, and branch or revision scope is
described inconsistently. At the same time, the document claims that acceptance
can close the naming policy questions at lines 802-804. That closure claim is
premature.

# P1 Acceptance Blockers

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-grammar"></a>

## 1. The Canonical Grammar Reverses Its Inputs While Calling The Change A Refinement

**Evidence.** The one-page answer requires the canonical form to preserve the
filename suffix and structural delimiters (`draft2.sol56x.md`, lines 132-133).
The detailed grammar makes the exact stored path, including `.md`, canonical,
uses `rekon:/constitution/README.md#doc-constitution`, and demotes flattened
forms to aliases (lines 234-267). The OKF Concept ID is then classified as a
projection that removes `.md` (lines 633-635), and the canonical constitution's
qualified ID is deprecated (line 717). The disposition table nevertheless calls
this an "Adopt, refine" result (line 759).

The human ruling gives `constitution-naming-exploration-proposal4-grammar` as
the full name implied by location (`proposal4.glm53m.md`, lines 81-87). The
pre-draft2 companion is even more explicit: a regular knowledge artifact's
identity is its tree path with the Markdown suffix removed
(`proposal4.sol56x.md`, lines 500-508). OKF defines a Concept ID as the path
within the bundle with `.md` removed ([OKF v0.2, lines
79-81](https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/knowledge-catalog/blob/main/okf/SPEC.md#L79-L81)).

**Why this blocks acceptance.** Keeping the suffix may be defensible for a
multi-format Rekon namespace, and preserving delimiters avoids real collisions.
But it is a new identity decision. It gives an OKF-backed document a Rekon
canonical identity and a different OKF canonical Concept ID, then relies on the
per-plane narrowing of one-name to tolerate the duplication. It also rejects
the ruling's concrete flattened spelling. Neither change can be smuggled through
as implementation detail.

**Correction.** Present the choice explicitly to the human gate. Either adopt
the ruling and OKF form, with authority qualification around a suffixless
bundle-relative path and anchor, or adopt an exact-suffix Rekon identity while
stating that it intentionally diverges from OKF and revises the ruling's
spelling. In the latter case, define the OKF ID as a typed cross-modal alias and
explain why one-name permits it. Also specify escaping and canonicalization for
`#`, `:`, spaces, percent escapes, Unicode, and inherited upstream paths; the
current displayed grammar is not reversible for every allowed Git path.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-membership"></a>

## 2. The Repository-Root Namespace Has No Membership Rule

**Evidence.** Scope says not every file or transient observation becomes a
first-class concept (`draft2.sol56x.md`, lines 142-148). Knowledge identity
applies to "Rekon-native tree knowledge" (lines 213-224), and section
composition calls the document a frontmatter-bearing artifact (lines 287-290).
Elsewhere, placement itself is the knowledge mint (lines 410-412). The OKF
section says the repository-relative namespace may contain selected knowledge
and non-knowledge artifacts (lines 622-624), while the project declaration sets
the namespace root to the repository root (lines 685-701).

**Why this blocks acceptance.** The draft never states how selection happens.
It is therefore impossible to determine whether `AGENTS.md`, code, generated
files, symlinks, vendored paths, transcripts, and non-frontmatter Markdown are
knowledge identities, artifact identities, or merely paths beneath the same
root. "Claim-bearing presence" is useful prose but not an admission rule. The
derived current inventory promised at lines 660-674 and 778 cannot be derived
until membership is observable.

Carrier identity is similarly unclear. A symlink is an alternate route at lines
226-228, a no-new-identity exposure at line 433, and a channel or alias identity
at lines 506-510. A tombstone is a distinct deprecated artifact at line 477.
Those can be coherent categories, but only if the namespace says which carrier
path names which entity kind.

**Correction.** Separate repository paths into at least two explicit classes:
tracked artifact identity and admitted knowledge identity. State whether every
tracked regular file receives an artifact identity, then define how a subset
becomes knowledge, such as frontmatter, a declared root-and-exclusion contract,
or a per-kind declaration. Define symlink, generated exposure, channel, and
tombstone membership in that same rule. This can remain derivable without a
central registry.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-authority"></a>

## 3. The Cross-Project Form Does Not Canonically Identify Its Authority

**Evidence.** The proposed spelling is
`<authority>:<root-relative-path>[#<explicit-anchor>]`
(`draft2.sol56x.md`, lines 239-243). The concrete authority is the alias `rekon`,
which the draft admits is only locally registered and is not evidence of global
ownership (lines 263-267). A cross-project citation merely `SHOULD` pair that
identity with a repository or bundle URL. The application repeats `rekon` as
the authority alias and lists the GitHub URL separately (lines 687-696). Durable
authority discovery and any serialized authority form beyond `rekon:` are
deferred at lines 812-815.

**Why this blocks acceptance.** Two unrelated projects can both call themselves
`rekon`, producing identical purportedly qualified strings. An optional locator
beside the string does not become part of its identity. The draft therefore has
a sound federation principle but no cross-project canonical reference, despite
claiming at lines 523-528 that cross-project identity is federated authority
plus native identity. This leaves the surviving request for a project namespace
within a wider namespace (`ask-audit0.glm53m.md`, line 68) unresolved.

**Correction.** Distinguish the durable authority identifier from its local
alias and current locator. Until a stronger authority form exists, make the
canonical repository or bundle URI mandatory in cross-project references, with
`rekon` explicitly an elision. Authority discovery can remain deferred; the
identity syntax cannot. Define how authority URIs and path fragments are encoded
without overloading an unescaped colon.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-actors"></a>

## 4. The Draft Reverses The Agent-Naming Flagship Without Naming That Revision

**Evidence.** The draft deliberately mints no actor persona and says model and
session provenance are enough (lines 102-104). The census names actors only
when future addressability matters (line 357). Actor self-naming is optional,
and separate executions remain unnamed instances unless later continuity
pressure arises (lines 564-574). The disposition table rejects "Actor persona
minting is required for accountability" at line 774.

The actual human asks were not that every artifact must carry a poetic persona.
They were that agents get names relating to existing names, that the root
session self-names and can change its name, and that numerical and Docker-style
names may coexist (`ask-audit0.glm53m.md`, lines 67-71). The ask audit records
all of these as surviving. Producer, session, actor continuity, role, and hat
answer different questions, as the reviewed draft itself correctly states at
lines 550-562.

**Why this blocks acceptance.** Replacing actor identity with production
metadata discards the original flagship while attacking a stronger ceremony
claim that the human did not make. Whether this particular non-root review or
draft author needs a persona is separate from whether the compact preserves the
agent-naming direction.

**Correction.** Add an explicit disposition for each actor ask. Preserve the
root self-name requirement and the numerical-plus-chosen-name pair unless the
human revises them. Define when durable-producing child agents receive instance
names, while retaining reference pressure for ephemeral executions. Keep
`generated.by`, session locator, actor name, hats, and role relations separate.
If the intended settlement is genuinely optional actor naming, label it as a
revision requiring human acceptance rather than a consequence of ceremony
budget.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-ideas"></a>

## 5. The Entity Census Does Not Name Durable Abstract Ideas

**Evidence.** The draft says a location identity names a claim-bearing presence
but not an abstract idea shared across documents (lines 150-153). It postpones
an independently governed concept entity until later reference pressure (lines
314-324). Its restatement of the original ambition lists knowledge, agents,
humans, events, findings, research, issues, computations, and artifacts, but
silently omits ideas (lines 343-367).

The ticket asks how to give enduring one-per-entity names to every actor,
artifact, and idea (`.beads/issues.jsonl`, line 15). The founding center likewise
includes knowledge, agents, humans, events, findings, research, issues, and
computations as named entities (`proposal2.glm53m.md`, lines 104-123).

**Why this blocks acceptance.** The draft correctly refuses to infer Platonic
sameness from similar prose. That does not answer how an already durable idea,
standard, or shared concept becomes referable when several claim-bearing
presences discuss it. "MAY later" leaves the census incomplete while the draft
claims policy closure.

**Correction.** Choose a proportional current rule. One minimal option is that
a maintained document or section can be explicitly declared the canonical
presence for a durable abstract concept, while evidence artifacts remain
distinct and relate to it. Another is a separate concept entity earned by
reference pressure. Whichever rule is chosen, define its authority and lifecycle
now, or explicitly revise the ticket to exclude abstract ideas from this
acceptance.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-rename-gate"></a>

## 6. The Rename Covenant Weakens The Human Gate

**Evidence.** A durable knowledge rename only `SHOULD` record its migration
facts (`draft2.sol56x.md`, lines 440-454). The list includes an approving
authority and revision but no human gate. The draft's definition of authority
includes native systems as well as stewards (line 161), so approval by an
authority is not equivalent to human approval.

The ruling says moves are human-gated, preceded by an inbound inventory, and
followed by compatibility stubs where durable consumers exist
(`proposal4.glm53m.md`, lines 88-95). Hierarchy1 similarly says the inventory is
assembled before movement (`README-heirarchy1.glm53m.md`, lines 352-370), even
though the rest of that hierarchy remains unaccepted.

**Why this blocks acceptance.** Rename cost is one of the four settled choices.
With `SHOULD`, a canonical path or anchor can change while remaining nominally
compliant without either sealed input or human approval. That changes the
ruling's liability from a gate into advice.

**Correction.** Make human approval and a revision-bound inbound inventory
`MUST` requirements for durable canonical path, anchor, namespace-root, and
authority changes. Keep compatibility conditional on observed consumers, but
require an explicit compatibility or loud-break decision. If low-pressure or
uncited files are exempt, define why they are not durable canonical entities
rather than weakening the covenant for all renames.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-anchor-invariant"></a>

## 7. The Anchor Decision Generalizes Beyond Its Measurement

**Evidence.** The draft requires unique rendered IDs and rejects equality
between explicit anchor and generated heading slug as a correctness rule (lines
292-307). It repeats that rejection in the disposition table at line 764. The
verification record correctly demonstrates that the prior synthesis's current
Pandoc representation emits duplicate IDs when a separate explicit anchor
equals an automatically identified heading (lines 834-845).

The ask audit records "Anchor equals section name, GFM text implies link implies
text" as a surviving request (`ask-audit0.glm53m.md`, lines 81-83). The human
ruling also lists slug-at-mint among what survives (`proposal4.glm53m.md`, lines
193-201).

**Why this blocks acceptance.** The experiment disproves one source-to-HTML
representation and the earlier synthesis's claimed verification. It does not
prove that the semantic anchor and section name must differ. A renderer or
source convention that emits one ID could preserve the requested invariant.
Conversely, if cross-renderer constraints make that impossible, the human ask
needs explicit revision. The new `MUST` is also not assessable until the
supported renderer set is declared; host-specific behavior is deferred at line
819.

**Correction.** Separate three requirements: stable semantic section identity,
semantic correspondence between anchor and visible heading, and uniqueness in
each supported rendered output. Retain the measured Pandoc result as a defect in
the old procedure. Then either choose a single-ID representation that preserves
the human invariant or ask the human to accept the prefixed-anchor alternative.
Name the renderers and publication carriers against which uniqueness is
normative.

## 8. The Request And Acceptance Ledger Is Incomplete

**Evidence.** The draft promises that every inherited claim is retained,
narrowed, deferred, or rejected (lines 83-87). Its one-page decision table
covers center, one-name, grammar, census, supersession, Beads, OKF, and tooling
(lines 126-138), and its inherited-claim disposition spans lines 754-779. It
then says acceptance should close the naming policy questions (lines 802-804).

Those sections never explicitly dispose of several requests recorded as
surviving in `ask-audit0.glm53m.md`, lines 71-83: the numerical plus Docker-name
pair, research/design role prefixes, optional model qualification, C2-style
short and long references, and multiple anchors per entity. The anchor equality
ask is rejected without being identified as a revision of a surviving human
request. The "Supersede ambition" answer at line 135 describes truthful lineage
relations, while the founding ambition was strategic: become the new core and
replace existing authority where earned (`proposal2.glm53m.md`, lines 316-333).

**Why correction is required.** The ticket's acceptance criterion is not simply
to produce a coherent architecture. It requires the center, one-name discipline,
grammar, census, and supersede ambition to be accepted or revised explicitly.
An omission cannot be inferred as a deferral after the document promises full
disposition.

**Correction.** Add a compact ask-disposition table keyed to all rows in the
ask audit. State `retain`, `revise`, `defer`, or `reject`, with the resulting
rule and rationale. Distinguish the mechanics of successor relations from the
strategic decision about which current constitution material this module earns
the right to supersede.

## 9. The Acceptance Change Performs Work Assigned To A Separate Plan

**Evidence.** The proposed acceptance change would synthesize the maintained

update the ticket (`draft2.sol56x.md`, lines 783-796). Only afterward would it

The `rekon-con-naming` acceptance criterion says: "If accepted, a separate

**Why correction is required.** The draft moves constitution amendment into

**Correction.** Let the acceptance record choose the compact and update the

change is now desired, revise the ticket criterion explicitly before treating
that sequence as accepted.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-source-identity"></a>

## 10. The Census Confuses An OKF Source Key With Source Identity

**Evidence.** The entity census says a source's natural identity is a keyed
`sources[].id` scoped to one document (`draft2.sol56x.md`, line 360). Later, the
draft correctly says that `sources` records provenance rather than peer identity
(line 651).

OKF requires `sources[].resource` within each source entry; that value names the
artifact or source scope. `sources[].id` is optional and is recommended when
the body needs a stable per-claim attribution key ([OKF v0.2, lines
303-313](https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/knowledge-catalog/blob/main/okf/SPEC.md#L303-L313)).
Its purpose is to survive source-list reordering, not to become the source's
canonical identity (lines 350-364).

**Why correction is required.** Treating a document-local citation key as the
source entity's natural identity permits the same source to receive unrelated
identities in each citing document and directly contradicts the draft's native
authority principle.

**Correction.** Rename the row to "source citation key" and classify
`sources[].id` as a scoped citation token. Treat `sources[].resource`, qualified
by its owning system where needed, as the source locator or native identity.
Do not claim that every source URI is trustworthy or canonical; it is the value
OKF requires for following the source.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-okf"></a>

## 11. The OKF Profile Contains Incorrect Or Ambiguous Claims

**Evidence.** The review target uses `stale_after: 2026-12-04` at line 8. OKF
requires every timestamp-valued key to be an ISO 8601 datetime with an explicit
UTC offset and defines `stale_after` as an absolute instant ([OKF v0.2, lines
284-285](https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/knowledge-catalog/blob/main/okf/SPEC.md#L284-L285)
and [424-433](https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/knowledge-catalog/blob/main/okf/SPEC.md#L424-L433)).

The target also says a future conformant bundle must declare root, membership,
packaging, symlink treatment, generated-file treatment, and treatment of
non-Markdown artifacts (lines 626-632). OKF itself defines a bundle as a
directory tree, permits several distribution forms, and has no such declaration
requirements (lines 109-149 and 736-763). These may be valuable Rekon profile
requirements, but they are not OKF conformance conditions.

Finally, the draft says a `human:` prefix states a "responsibility class" under
OKF (lines 580-582). OKF defines it as the actor convention for a person and
uses the prefix to derive human-reviewed trust ([lines
489-501](https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/knowledge-catalog/blob/main/okf/SPEC.md#L489-L501)).
The draft is right that the string does not authenticate the person.

**Correction.** Use an offset-bearing `stale_after` instant. Label bundle-root,
membership, packaging, and carrier declarations as Rekon's stricter export
profile, not OKF requirements. Replace "responsibility class" with "person actor
class used for trust-tier classification," while retaining the warning that the
prefix is not authentication.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-current-exact"></a>

## 12. Canonical Line, Canonical Revision, Channel, And Exact Evidence Conflict

**Evidence.** The one-page discipline scopes identity by native plane,
authority, and "canonical line" (line 131), while the vocabulary says an
authority assigns a name "on its canonical line" (line 162). The identity tuple
contains only authority, path, and optional anchor (lines 215-219). A default
branch is separately classified as a mutable channel at line 168. The detailed
one-name rule then changes the scope to "canonical revision" at lines 371-376,
although exact historical evidence requires an immutable revision at lines
328-339. The application declares `main` as canonical public line at line 692.

The revision-binding definition also says a "commit, change, receipt,
signature, or other coordinate" fixes an exact observation (line 166), and the
reference-family table says evidence binding "prove[s]" the observation at line
386. If "change" includes a jj change ID, it does not identify immutable bytes:
a jj change persists across rewritten commits. A commit ID binds exact tree
state; neither a path nor a commit alone proves the truth of a claim.

**Why correction is required.** It is unclear whether `main` participates in
identity, selects the current owner, or is merely a resolution channel. Scoping
one-name per revision would also weaken the prohibition on silently reusing a
path for unrelated meaning, because every revision could acquire a different
owner.

**Correction.** Keep the canonical tuple as authority plus path plus anchor.
Define the canonical publication line as the authority's mutable selection
channel, not another identity component. Reserve exact evidence for a canonical
repository identifier plus immutable commit ID plus path and anchor at that
commit. Replace generic "change" with the exact identifier kind intended, and
replace "prove" with "binds or identifies" except where a signature or attester
actually establishes a specified proposition.

<a id="naming-draft2-review0-measurements"></a>

## 13. The Raw Measurements Are True But Do Not Prove The Broader Claims

**Evidence.** The draft says that parent revision
`e0816af9271990ac50bb72c95175f834f1b8c888` contains 129 tracked Markdown paths
and that 79 have no line beginning `type:` (lines 618-624). Those raw counts
reproduce at that revision. However, the displayed commands at lines 847-859 do
not include the revision. Run against the later worktree, they produce 130 and
79. The `git grep -L` test also does not parse frontmatter or check whether
`type` is non-empty. Its 79 paths include the reserved `doc/log.md`, where a
Concept `type` is not required, and four Markdown-named symlinks whose OKF
treatment the draft correctly says is unspecified.

The conclusion that the repository root is not currently an OKF-conformant
bundle is still supportable: ordinary non-reserved files such as the root
README lack Concept frontmatter. The number 79 is not a count of conformance
violations.

The draft also says no Markdown links outside the naming subtree point into it
(lines 747-750). Its evidence searches only the literal text
`constitution/naming` (lines 856-859). A relative link from `constitution/`, an
HTML link, or another equivalent spelling would not match. The command proves
the absence of that literal occurrence, not all resolved inbound Markdown
links.

**Correction.** Pin every evidence command to the named revision. Parse the
frontmatter block at file start, classify reserved names and symlinks before
counting failures, and report raw counts separately from conformance judgments.
For inbound inventory, parse and resolve Markdown and HTML destinations from
their source directories rather than grepping one spelling. Retain the draft's
valuable warning that migration inventories must be sealed to a revision.

## 14. Repetition Obscures Which Formulation Is Normative

The native-home and no-universal-registry thesis appears in the introduction
(lines 89-100), one-page decision (lines 126-138), native-authority section
(lines 178-195), census and one-name sections (lines 343-412), federation
section (lines 521-546), and inherited-claim disposition (lines 754-779).
Rename policy similarly appears in the change table and covenant (lines
416-464), compatibility section (lines 466-489), disposition table (lines
754-779), and acceptance contract (lines 783-804).

This is not merely long-form explanation. The repetitions vary terms such as
project root, namespace root, bundle root, canonical line, canonical revision,
alias identity, and channel identity. Those variations contribute directly to
the substantive ambiguities above.

**Correction.** Keep one short normative compact organized as authority and
membership, canonical identities, reference forms, change, and acceptance.
Move rationale, corpus measurements, inherited-claim disposition, and Rekon
application after it. Let the ask-disposition table point to normative clauses
rather than restating them. The result can retain evidence and nuance while
making contradictions easier to detect.

1. Obtain a human decision on suffixless/flattened ruling grammar versus the
   draft's exact-suffix structured grammar.
2. Define namespace membership and carrier identity before deriving indexes or
   promising collision checks.
3. Define a durable cross-project authority form, leaving discovery mechanics
   deferred if necessary.
