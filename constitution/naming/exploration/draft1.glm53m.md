<a id="why-the-evidence-favors-the-ruling"></a>

## Why The Evidence Favors The Ruling

The syntheses converged under a shared corpus and a shared taste for
move-stable stems. The ruling's challenge — was the settlement a
preference priced as law? — is answerable with this corpus's own cost
ledger, and the ledger is lopsided:

- **Stem collisions paid, repeatedly.** The five-draft wave minted five
  promise-law variants, five threshold variants, three successor variants;
  weaver's ledger resolves the clusters. The synthesis round then produced
  the cleanest specimen of all: `naming-tool-seam` and
  `naming-tooling-seam`, minted for one concept by two independent
  syntheses on the same afternoon, caught and resolved by hand in
  proposal3b. Collision policing consumed real effort in every round.
- **Location renames paid, approximately never.** In this effort's whole
  recorded life, no canonical knowledge file has moved. The one rename
  event the chain absorbed — proposal0's `agent-naming-*` family re-minted
  as `naming-*` — was a stem rename under the syntheses' own law, not a
  path move, and it was absorbed by hand precisely because stems, not
  paths, were the identity layer.
- **The contemplated move is one migration, inventoried below.** The only
  location-identity change on the horizon — promoting
  `constitution/naming/` to a root topic — is a single human-gated
  migration whose full cost I measured (next section): it is a day of
  mechanical work, not a structural hazard.

A law should be priced against observed costs. Codex said this in the
addendum and I confirm it with numbers: for tree-resident knowledge in
this corpus, location identity is the settlement the evidence selects. I
also note the census vindication, which nobody has drawn: proposal3's
speech-act table already said a knowledge name is an **address** — where a
meaning lives. The syntheses then gave knowledge minted stems while
reserving addresses for the register that "carries no meaning," an
internal contradiction; the ruling returns the census row to literal truth.

<a id="what-changes-in-the-surviving-structures"></a>

## What Changes In The Surviving Structures

The companion and the three addenda have rewritten the affected tables
(registers name row, identity-change move rows). Confirmed, not repeated.
Three structural consequences deserve statement because they will shape
the successor document:

1. **The full name is two-tone.** A section's identity composes a path
   stratum (governed by migration law: moves are renames, paid by
   `naming-rename-covenant` and `naming-convergence-repair`) with an
   anchor stratum (governed by `naming-slug-at-mint`: heading edits never
   rename, because the anchor was pinned at birth). The two strata have
   different change procedures, different costs, and different tools. This
   is `naming-placement-at-mint` stated as structure: placement declares
   the document grain, the pinned anchor declares the section grain, and
   neither stratum borrows the other's mutability.
2. **The repair machinery is promoted from safety net to standing
   procedure.** Under the syntheses, `naming-rename-covenant` and
   `naming-convergence-repair` guarded against hypothetical moves. Under
   the ruling, every layout decision is an identity decision, so the
   covenant is the ordinary payment procedure — the successor's chapter
   on change is now load-bearing law rather than insurance.
3. **The stem ledger changes job, not status.** Identifier ledgers were
   collision police under minted-stem law; under location law the tree
   polices local collisions for free. The ledger's remaining work is
   corpus-scale: recording same-concept relations across documents (five
   drafts, one promise-law idea, five section identities). Codex said the
   ledger "is the relation maintenance that location identity also
   requires"; I add that this is exactly the declared-reference-family
   obligation, so the ledger practice survives as the corpus-scale
   register, kept by hand until pressure earns tooling.

## The Rename Ripple Priced Against Real Files

The ruling accepts the ripple as deliberate cost. Here is what it actually
costs in this corpus, measured during this run. All counts were taken
directly from the working tree; concurrent edits by sibling agents are
landing, so treat these as today's floor, not eternal truth.

### The Promotion Move Priced

The move the whole chain contemplates at acceptance:
`constitution/naming/` promoted to a root topic, executed with
hierarchy1's migration machinery.

| Quantity | Measured | Work it creates |
|---|---|---|
| Documents moved | 16 | 16 document identities renamed |
| Pinned anchors inside them | 401 | 401 section identities renamed by composition |
| Internal root-relative links | 134 | mechanical same-subtree rewrite, one reviewable change |

The total bill: 417 location identities renamed, 134 mechanical link
rewrites, 39 frontmatter fields dispositioned, 2 work-plane records
repaired — and a compatibility surface at the old directory only if the
epic's deep forward anchor is worth preserving rather than updating. This
is a bounded, reviewable migration. The ruling's economy holds on real
numbers: the ceremony the subtree spent minting and policing 400-plus
independent stems exceeded, in effort already paid, the entire price of
this move.

<a id="the-floor-price-of-a-typo-correction"></a>

### The Floor Price Of A Typo Correction

The corpus's standing embarrassment — `README-heirarchy1.glm53m.md`,
misspelled — is now also its cheapest measured rename. Correcting the
spelling would rename one document identity and its 19 sections (20
identities), and would orphan 55 inbound string references across 13
files, every one of them inside the naming subtree. The interesting fact
is where those references live: in **frozen wave evidence** — committed
drafts, proposals, syntheses — which the constitution says not to rewrite.
The cheapest honest payment is therefore not 55 rewrites of evidence but a
compatibility stub at the old path (a symlink to the corrected name),
exactly the pattern hierarchy1 names and the retired OpenCode `plugin.md`
demonstrates. Generalized, this is a pricing rule the successor should
carry: **inbound references held by frozen evidence are paid with stubs,
not rewrites; inbound references held by living documents are paid with
rewrites; the migration map states which class each reference is in.**
The syntheses' covenant records four facts; this adds the fifth — the
payer's class.

<a id="the-external-surface-today"></a>

### The External Surface Today

The ruling's loudest feared cost — external citations rotting in other
people's documents — has, measured today, **zero payers into this
subtree**. More broadly, the entire live external citation surface into
rekon is five links, all `file://`-absolute, all in the
`~/archive/doc` corpus: four from `jj.md` into `vcs/jj/` and one from
`opencode/patches.md` into `code/task-reconstruction/README.md`. And the
federation story — project-qualified names resolved across authorities —
has **zero live instances anywhere**: no document outside this repository
cites a rekon entity in qualified form, and no rekon document cites an
external project's knowledge by qualified name rather than `file://` path.

Two consequences. First, weaver's held tension (the external citation
surface is borne by third parties) is correct as a standing promise but is
currently a bet, not a bill — the ruling's external cost is real only when
federation becomes practice. Second, the first genuinely federated
citation will set precedent unguided; the successor should write the form
before the first instance ships (see
[cross project citation](#cross-project-citation)). Note also that the
existing external links are workspace-absolute — they break on repository
relocation, not on file moves — so location identity neither worsens nor
improves today's external fragility.

<a id="what-everyone-missed"></a>

## What Everyone Missed

Five findings the ruling, the companion, the three addenda, and the audit
do not contain. The first three change practice; the last two are
measurements that change posture.

<a id="directory-grain-naming"></a>

### Directory Grain Naming

Under location identity plus this workspace's module law (README.md is
canonical), the Concept ID of most canonical knowledge terminates in the
string `README`: `constitution/README`, `vcs/jj/README`,
`design/doc-constitution/README`. The terminal segment is a
convention-mandated constant carrying zero concern semantics — the
semantic name is carried entirely by the **directory**, and `README` is to
directories what `.md` is to files: a format suffix. Three consequences:

1. **The identity unit and the module unit coincide.** A directory with
   one README is one named concept with sections — exactly hierarchy1's
   topic-first hierarchy, now grounded as identity rather than layout
   taste.
2. **Directory renames are the canonical renames.** Renaming a file
   beneath a stable directory is a cheap terminal-segment change; renaming
   a directory multiplies across every descendant identity. Layout review
   under the ruling should spend its care on directory names first.
3. **README elision is a legitimate resolution form already covered by
   law.** `naming-contextual-elision` permits removing what context
   restores, and a directory containing a README restores the terminal
   segment by rule. So `constitution/README` and `constitution` may
   denote the same concept where the context declares the module
   convention — no new mechanism, just the register operating. I propose
   the successor name this elision explicitly rather than leave readers
   to discover it.

<a id="self-reference-drift"></a>

### Self Reference Drift

Measured: all 16 files in the naming subtree carry a `resource:` field
naming their own path, 16 carry `local_knowledge_id`, and 7 carry
`qualified_knowledge_id`. Under minted-stem law these fields were arguably
the identity carriers. Under location identity each one is a **redundant
projection of the identity the tree already declares** — and a drift
liability: move the file without editing frontmatter and the file carries
a second, stale, self-asserted name, which is precisely the
duplicate-drift defect the ruling left standing. The companion's inventory
lists these fields among the references to repair, but the deeper
obligation is unstated: a file's self-reference must either go or be
mechanically true. The successor should adopt, and the deferred
`naming-tool-seam` should eventually check: **`resource` equals own path,
or `resource` is absent.** The `local_knowledge_id` and
`qualified_knowledge_id` fields become what the companion called
compatibility and indexing projections — the successor should say which
one (at most) survives, so the self-reference surface stops being three
fields wide.

<a id="the-accepted-tip-inversion"></a>

### The Accepted Tip Inversion

AGENTS.md's accepted-tip convention — make a prefix-less symlink for the
accepted wave file — collides with the ruling in a way nobody has
resolved. The corpus already contains the live specimen:
`design/doc-constitution/adoption.md -> adoption1.glm53m.md`, and the
constitution's cross-references cite the **symlink path** as the accepted
adoption record. Under location identity this is an inversion: the
canonical, cited knowledge presence is a path that owns no regular
content, while the regular file — which the companion's table makes the
declared owner — carries a model-suffixed name that exists for exact
evidence, not for citation. One entity, two paths, and the question of
which path is the name is answered by corpus practice (the suffix-less
one) but by no law.

The norm, minted as `naming-acceptance-placement`: **acceptance is itself
a placement act.** The accepted knowledge identity is minted at the
suffix-less path — by symlink exposure with declared precedence, or by an
actual move with the model-suffixed path retired to evidence. The
model-suffixed regular file remains the exact-evidence identity
(`naming-derived-vs-declared` kinship: the wave file is the observation,
the tip is the pinned account). This bends the companion's "regular file
placement declares ownership" by one honest degree — body ownership and
cited-name ownership can sit at different paths when a symlink declares
the mapping and the precedence — and it should be stated as the norm
rather than left to be inferred from one stray specimen in
`design/doc-constitution/`.

<a id="the-bundle-root-is-undeclared"></a>

### The Bundle Root Is Undeclared

OKF §2 grounds the Concept ID in "the path of the concept's file **within
the bundle**," and §3 defines the bundle as a directory tree. Rekon
adopted the path rule by ruling and never declared its bundle. Every
identity in this corpus is therefore relative to an unstated base. The
norm, minted as `naming-bundle-root`: **the repository working copy is the
knowledge bundle; root-relative `/` links are bundle-relative; and the
non-knowledge trees inside the repository — `.jj/`, `.git/`, `.beads/`,
`.test-agent/`, build output — are inside the repository but outside the
knowledge namespace**, so location identity does not attach to them (the
work plane's records keep their native beads identity under
`naming-native-authority`). Two adjacent facts: the workspace's second
knowledge bundle, `~/archive/doc`, is a separate jj repository, and the
inter-bundle citation form in current practice is the `file://` absolute
path — acceptable for local WIP per the constitution, and the thing a
future federated form would replace. Declaring the base costs one sentence
and removes an entire class of "relative to what?" ambiguity from the
successor.

<a id="federation-has-no-live-instances"></a>

### Federation Has No Live Instances

Stated above as measurement, restated as miss: three syntheses, two
overrides, and an audit debate the federation boundary — authority
discovery, cross-authority moves, qualified forms — while the corpus
contains **zero federated citations**. The named-system ambition
("implicitly across projects") is currently carried entirely by
co-location in one workspace and `file://` links. This does not weaken the
federation law (`naming-federated-authority` is right and its costs are
correctly stated); it re-weights it: federation is the system's largest
untested surface, and the ruling's two genuinely unpriced bets — external
breakage and standalone travel — both live there. Watch items, not
reopenings.

<a id="tensions-the-ruling-creates-or-sharpens"></a>

## Tensions The Ruling Creates Or Sharpens

<a id="standalone-travel-across-trees"></a>

### Standalone Travel Across Trees

Codex's `naming-extraction-asymmetry` — full location names land exactly
and travel heavy; stems travel light and land only via a carried map — is
the right law, and the corpus's own practice suggests the map's form: the
OKF frontmatter **sources table is a portable map**. Every key in it
carries resource, title, and author; a traveling document that keeps its
sources table exact keeps its cross-tree references recoverable, and
proposal3b's identifier ledger is the same device at corpus grain. The
practice point for the successor: a document written to travel (the codex
mandate) should be required to carry its map in its frontmatter, because
the map is the only part of its reference family that survives extraction
intact. Whether plain-markdown practice sustains map-keeping is codex's
held tension; I share it and add only that the failure mode is loud (a
dangling stem with no map is visibly unresolved, not silently rebound).

<a id="cross-project-citation"></a>

### Cross Project Citation

Weaver's held tension — external breakage lands on parties who did not
choose the move — is the correct standing promise. My addition is the
precedent gap: since no federated citation exists, the **first one will be
written by whoever needs it, unprimed**, and will likely be a `file://`
path or a bare stem, both of which the ruling's law would call defective.
The successor should pre-write the form now, while it costs nothing: a
federated citation pairs the qualified name with a canonical repository
URL and exact path (the constitution's cross-project rule already says
this in prose); under the ruling it additionally means the citation
**embeds a foreign tree path** and the citing author accepts the cited
authority's migration discipline sight unseen. That last clause is the
ruling's real federation cost, and it should be stated in the successor's
citation law, not discovered at first use.

<a id="section-identity-against-okf-document-identity"></a>

### Section Identity Against OKF Document Identity

The companion is honest that OKF settles document grain and rekon's
`path#anchor` is an extension. Quantified, the extension is nearly the
whole system: the naming subtree holds 16 document identities and 401
section identities — **over 96 percent of the named world lives at section
grain**, which OKF does not name at all. Three consequences. First, the
extension must be described as an extension everywhere it is claimed
(`naming-work-knowledge-seam` discipline applied to standards: do not let
"OKF-aligned" silently cover the fragment). Second, the anchor stratum
inherits full identity weight, so `naming-slug-at-mint` and mechanical
verification are not style law but identity law — the cross-renderer
conformance probe the corpus keeps deferring gates the validity of 96
percent of its names. Third, fragment portability across publication
surfaces (does a symlinked path preserve `#fragment` on each renderer?
does GitHub honor explicit `id` anchors exactly?) remains unverified; it
stays at the tool seam, now with a sharper reason to be probed early.

<a id="hat-scope-across-efforts"></a>

### Hat Scope Across Efforts

The ruling restored hats; the round then immediately produced the case
study: **four actors wore the hat `respondent` in one afternoon** —
weaver, concordance, codex, and now me — because each answered the same
ruling in its own artifact. No collision occurred, and the reason is the
missing sentence: a hat's mapping is declared in the actor's own artifact
and resolves **within that artifact**; across artifacts, the hat must be
qualified (`respondent` of weaver, or weaver-as-respondent). Hats are
artifact-scoped resolution forms, not workspace-scoped names — the
companion's "hat scope collisions" tension, now with a live instance and a
clean rule. The successor should write the scope sentence; nothing more is
needed, and a hat registry would be premature ceremony under
`naming-ceremony-budget`.

<a id="does-anything-need-new-law"></a>

## Does Anything Need New Law

Mostly no. The ruling is law; the surviving corpus carries the rest. What
is genuinely owed is small and derivative:

| Owed | Form | Status here |
|---|---|---|
| Declare the bundle root | one normative sentence | minted: `naming-bundle-root` |
| Acceptance as a placement act | one norm + precedence rule | minted: `naming-acceptance-placement` |
| Self-reference hygiene (`resource` equals own path or absent) | tool-seam check, successor sentence | proposed, no mint — it is an application of the duplicate-drift finding |
| README terminal-segment elision | successor sentence under `naming-contextual-elision` | proposed, no mint — register application |
| Evidence-class pricing rule for the ripple | successor sentence extending `naming-rename-covenant` | proposed, no mint — fifth covenant fact |
| Federated citation form | successor sentence, written before first use | proposed, no mint |

Explicitly **not** new law: alias governance (earned by pressure, per the
ruling), a hat registry (artifact scope suffices), a corpus-scale stem
registry (the hand-kept ledger is the register until it rots; governance
is then earned — codex's held tension, which I answer by declining to
pre-pay it), and any revival of move-stable stems (the preference is
priced and declined). The `naming-tool-seam` backlog inherits two new
items from this document: the self-reference check and the fragment
portability probe.

<a id="order-and-nebula"></a>

## Order And Nebula

Settled by this round, in my judgment: location identity for tree-resident
knowledge; the rename ripple as priced, deliberate cost; hats as declared
artifact-scoped aliases; cross-modal aliases as precedent under the
