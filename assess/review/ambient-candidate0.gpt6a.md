## Assessment praxis

- Become **well equipped for the next decision** before making it. Assessment
  connects purpose, architectural fit, close evidence, and reflection. Explain
  what to preserve as well as what to question; review is not permission to
  rewrite. [Method and case study](/assess/README.md).
- Declare a wave's inputs, baseline, artifact owners, independence rule, and
  release condition before dispatch. For independent comparison, save the
  parent's own review before reading peers and request **receipts, not findings**
  in completion notifications.
- A **full-wave gate** waits for all declared results; a **dependency gate**
  waits for the prerequisites of a particular next task. Do not silently turn
  all-results into a quorum. Missing participants and failures need explicit
  disposition, not a claim of completion.
- Separate **arrival, readiness, and authorization**: collect the inputs, read
  the complete required evidence set before comparing it, then authorize the
  next action. Keep independent work moving; use notifications, not polling.
  If nothing independent remains, yield with the gate closed. [Gate rationale
  and exceptions](/assess/gates/README.md).
- When evaluating a prompt or procedure, name that as an explicit deliverable,
  not an afterthought. Judge observed behavior against the ask, including
  misdirection, omissions, cost, and what the experiment cannot establish.

## Work history and capability maps

- Make architectural epics self-explaining: why the capability matters, its
  boundaries and relationships, and observable completion. Children describe
  coherent changes, not one ticket per file. Closed work need not remain open
  merely to represent an existing module.
- Map consequential capabilities, including CLI flags, to their actual owner
  and evidence. Distinguish implemented, verified under a stated environment,
  proposed, and not found. A flag's existence does not prove downstream use.
- A cross-session **happening** can be a bounded explanatory epic: origin,
  native sessions/commits, related work, changes in understanding, and outcome.
  Preserve lineage across directory moves. Chronology is not a blocking edge.
- “Tickets are the changelog” should derive history from native work records,
  not introduce a competing lifecycle. State retention and attribution limits;
  current-state JSONL is not an event log. Generated projections must not
  recursively become their own source material.
