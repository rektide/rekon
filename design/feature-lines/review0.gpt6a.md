<a id="thesis"></a>
# First assessment: strengthen the return path, not just the filing system

**Rekon is already becoming a place where working experience can change how we
think and act. I would preserve that widening, not discipline it back into an
efficient instruction manual.** The strongest new documents do more than tell an
agent what to avoid: they make purpose discussable, preserve alternatives, and
connect a real encounter with a better next attempt.

My interpretation is that the principal pressure is **continuity across those
encounters**: the human's meaning reaches an agent; the agent's interpretation
reaches a document; a later reader reaches a maintained account; actual use
returns a correction. Rekon has strong explanations of nearly every part of
this loop. The reported session and the directly observed rediscovery below
suggest that having those explanations is not yet the same as traversing them.

That is not an argument against more ideas or more documents. It is an argument
for making the accumulated ideas easier to **re-enter, contest, and carry into
the next action**. A glossary can be part of this, but neither a glossary nor a
directory reorganization can by itself make an assistant hear a clarification.

### 4. Newness, selection, use, and truth are different coordinates—not one ladder

The corpus already says this remarkably often. [Digest](/digest/README.md)
(lines 35–46) separates recency from authority. [Checkpoint discipline](/code/commits.md)
(lines 35–66) separates saving from acceptance and actual instruction loading.
[Forge](/forge/README.md) (lines 198–221, 294–310) shows why merged, currently
usable, relevant, and supported are different facts. [Remake](/remake/README.md)
(lines 136–153) refuses to lend final-tree evidence to a proposed patch prefix.

For this discussion I would ask distinct questions rather than introduce a
universal document state machine:

| Coordinate | Reader's question | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Posted / captured | Has an account been made available here, to this audience? | Agreement, promotion, or actual uptake. “Posted” is descriptive language here, not a proposed metadata state. |
| Honing | Is someone deliberately trying and revising its expression? | That the text is inactive, or that it is accepted. |
| Accepted / promoted | Who selected what, for which scope or audience? | Factual verification, implementation, or deployment. |
| Exposed / selected for assembly | Through which surface is it offered? | That any particular host or reader actually loaded it. |
| Activated / used | Did it enter this task's conduct? | That its interpretation was faithful or its result useful. |
| Current | Current **for what**: continuation, accepted guidance, observed implementation, publication, or a particular baseline? | That it has the newest timestamp, or outranks every other current selection. |

This matters in both directions. A draft can influence a live action, and a
well-maintained, accepted account can be missed entirely. The workshop's
[manual assembly receipt](/design/rekon/context-weave/assembly0.gpt56s.md)
(lines 28–67) is careful about its sources and non-cutover status. Its readable
shape is evidence of a prepared view, not proof of instruction uptake.

<a id="suggestions"></a>
# Four high-leverage suggestions

These are options for discussion. They do not preselect an implementation pass.

## 1. Make a clarification capable of changing the frame

**Recommendation:** when the human corrects a term or describes an experience,
briefly preserve the correction in their own language and explain what changes
in the agent's interpretation before offering another choice menu. A new A/B/C
inside the old frame is not necessarily a response to the clarification.

The existing [README-driven loop](/praxis/readme-driven/README.md) (lines 50–74)
already gives this a practice home: recover intent, learn, negotiate, and revise.
The [naming override](/constitution/naming/exploration/proposal4.glm53m.md)
(lines 114–137) is a larger cautionary case: several models' convergence did
not justify demoting what the human wanted. It should remain visible precisely
because it is evidence of a failure mode, not just an old naming debate.

For glossarying, try a **meaning-bearing entry**, not merely a synonym:

> Term and scope → the distinction it helps us make → a small example and
> counterexample → related meanings that are not interchangeable → owning
> discussion or practice, including an unresolved choice if one remains.

This is a writing suggestion, not a required schema for every word. Convenient
aliases, evocative terms, and several schools of thought should remain possible.
The glossary should help a later reader recover the human's distinction, not
translate every interesting phrase into the assistant's preferred ontology.

## 2. Make the session's hand-back as useful as its new document

**Recommendation:** at a consequential checkpoint, say what the work changes in
the maintained understanding, where that understanding belongs, and what is
still awaiting human direction. A few lines in an existing home, or a proposed
update held for review, can do this. It need not become another report or ticket.

This extends the existing [doc-pass](/AGENTS.md) (lines 58–70), not a replacement
process. The missing question to emphasize is: **what meaning is now easier to
recover, and where will the next session encounter it?** Link correctness is
necessary but cannot answer that question by itself.

The ignored bookmark design makes this tangible. Preserve its relevant
distinctions when they become load-bearing, with honest provenance and its
unresolved gates. Do not simply promote its proposed implementation unchanged.
Likewise, a session may contribute an interesting alternative without changing
the maintained recommendation: a home can say why the alternative matters and
what would reopen it. That is cumulative learning, not unfinished housekeeping.

## 3. Exercise reader journeys and action boundaries—not only the inventory

**Recommendation:** select a few encounters from real work and ask whether
Rekon helps a fresh reader make the right distinction *before it matters*.
The parent and human should choose the encounters; feature lines are one
candidate, not the predetermined entire trial.

| Candidate encounter | What a useful trial could reveal |
|---|---|
| Find a tool for a desired experience, not just a literal feature name | Whether routing follows expressive intent and notices adjacent capabilities rather than treating a search miss as nonexistence. |
| Respond after the human changes or clarifies the frame | Whether the new understanding changes the next response instead of producing another polished version of the unwanted task. |
| Resume among an active line, dated references, and an accepted baseline | Whether the reader distinguishes continuation, preservation, acceptance, and forge defaults without a silent ref move. A read-only worked example can test this first. |
| Complete a scoped operation that crosses a reference boundary | Whether intended completion includes dependent references and unrelated state stays outside the action. The reported remote-HEAD and empty-commit episodes are candidate cases, not verified reproductions here. |
| Return an example's lesson to Rekon | Whether the next session finds the existing distinction, and whether the case changes guidance rather than merely furnishing it with an anecdote. |

The precedent is already strong: [skillification](/design/skillify/README1.sol56x.md)
(lines 115–125, 553–565, 579–635) treats routing, execution, and maintenance as
separate failure surfaces and measures the whole path. [Evidence's case-story
practice](/evidence/README.md) (lines 69–88) asks what help was expected and what
actually happened. Use those capabilities before inventing a new audit system.

Success should include **better possibilities noticed, a more faithful human
conversation, and fewer repeated recoveries of lost meaning**. Merely shrinking
the context or preventing unauthorized edits would miss much of Rekon's promise.

## 4. Reconcile relationships before reconciling the directory tree

**Recommendation:** discuss what constitution, praxis, shared vocabulary, and
domain homes should do for one another. Do not begin by choosing which directory
contains the others.

There are at least three useful editorial centers already present:

- **A shared compact:** names, source ownership, durable references, scoped
  authority, and the terms on which knowledge becomes something others rely on.
- **A living repertoire:** ways to look, choose, act, learn, and enlarge the
  possible; methods whose differences can remain fruitful.
- **An operational memory:** concrete commands, traps, examples, and boundaries
  that must be available when the work needs them.

These are interpretive roles, not proposed new modules or a constitutional
ranking. The present constitution itself includes a learning loop; praxis has
discipline and obligations; tool references can teach philosophy through an
incident. For example, the [jj guide's retained false-verification story](/vcs/jj/README.md)
(lines 274–281) is more educational than a bare rule about source checking.

The compact-context school in [skillification](/design/skillify/README1.sol56x.md)
(lines 349–378) and the richer hand-weaving school in the [north-star account](/design/rekon/context-weave/north-star0.gpt6a.md)
(lines 75–100) need not be averaged into a compromise paragraph. One emphasizes
reliable selection and cost of absence; the other deliberately tests repetition
as orientation. Explain which setting each serves and learn from their use.

<a id="glossary-options"></a>
## Global shared semantics, praxis, or constitution?

“Global” can mean **shared across topics**, **visible before specialized
reading**, or **actually delivered ambiently by a host**. These should not be
treated as one placement decision. A glossary can be globally useful without
being globally authoritative or loaded in full on every turn.

| Option | What it emphasizes | Useful cost or tension |
|---|---|---|
| **Strengthen the existing root `CONTEXT.md`, keep domain depth local** | Shared semantics as a reader service. Small local definitions and candidate GLOBALs link into it. | Lowest structural change; the entry should retain source/scope so short definitions do not quietly become law. This is already the workshop's shape. |
| **Constitution sponsors shared terminology; praxis sponsors glossarying through use** | The compact protects distinctions and reference obligations; lived examples teach and revise their expression. Existing paths can remain. | A promising interpretation of the human's analogy, but not yet a settled division of authority. Avoid turning every word correction into a constitutional amendment. |
| **A more explicitly independent vocabulary concern** | A durable cross-topic home with domain-grouped term families and links to competing usages, coequal in visibility with praxis. | Could make the spirit more legible, but risks a second glossary or a new umbrella that merely repeats the root. First establish what the existing surface cannot do. Any move changes names under the human ruling. |

My **provisional preference** is to try richer semantics through the existing
surface before moving it, while discussing the second option as a relationship
rather than a file plan. This is not a verdict against the third option or the
human's constitutional analogy. The desired social and intellectual role of the
glossary should choose its home, not the other way around.

<a id="feature-trial"></a>
## Feature lines and `prime`: a concrete possible trial, not the whole agenda

The commission supplies a firm distinction: **`prime` means the most recent,
active feature-line tip in the human's intended usage—not the forge default
branch.** Preserve that meaning before deciding its representation.

A candidate explanation could start: *“Prime names the active continuation tip
we mean for this feature line. Name the line and the tip; do not derive prime
from the default branch or from whichever timestamp sorts last.”* The exact
selection rule still needs the human. In particular, an in-progress rebuild can
be the active work while an accepted bookmark deliberately stays behind.

For a worked example, keep these separate:

| Thing | Distinction to preserve |
|---|---|
| Feature line | The identified development line under discussion, with its intent and baseline; not automatically the entire effort or every branch with a similar prefix. Whether dated incarnations are separate lines or references within one line is an open usage question. |
| Prime / active tip | The human's current continuation meaning. It might be represented by a bookmark or by an explicit documented selection; no actual name or mechanism is chosen here. |
| Dated feature reference | A dated name. Explain its role instead of assuming the spelling proves immutability or currentness. |
| Snapshot / checkpoint | A preserved state intended for later comparison or recovery. A mutable ref can implement a preservation convention; that does not make the underlying ref mechanically immutable. |
| Exact revision | The particular bytes/history basis cited. Neither a moving bookmark nor a jj change ID alone is a pin to one historical commit version. |
| Working copy | Where the current work is being edited; its revision may be empty or contain unrelated work. It is not automatically the prime feature tip. |
| Accepted feature reference / accepted document channel | Scoped selections for reliance, with different owners and promises. Neither must coincide with active construction. |
| Forge default branch | The forge's default entry or selection. It may point to prime by deliberate choice, but the two concepts are not synonyms. |

This could become a small linked glossary family and a domain-owned worked
example if selected. [Inventory row 22](/design/rekon/context-weave/inventory0.gpt56s.md)
(line 115; open seam at lines 261–263) already puts everyday policy near `code/`
and tool forensics in `vcs/jj/`. The earlier ignored design offers
`code/bookmarks.md` or a section in `code/commits.md`; those remain options, not
an accepted destination. No new `prime` ref, rename, branch deletion, forge
setting, GLOBAL activation, or vocabulary migration follows from this report.

<a id="next-discussion"></a>
# What I would discuss next

Before another pass, let the human and parent compare the live-session account
with this source reading. **Which failure most needs Rekon to help with next?**
Several can be selected, and a different one can be named:

- **Hearing and retaining the human's frame:** use the tool-search and
  clarification episodes to examine whether the next response actually changes.
- **Making shared words survive a new session:** use `prime` and the earlier
  bookmark design to trial a meaning-bearing glossary family.
- **Making completed actions whole and bounded:** examine reference dependencies,
  completion claims, and unrelated state, without performing those operations.
- **Keeping the wider map generative:** discuss the relationship between compact,
  repertoire, vocabulary, and operational memory, preserving useful schools of
  thought rather than converging prematurely on a directory plan.

If the vocabulary trial is chosen, the sharp local question is: **when a new
line is actively being built but the previously accepted reference has not
moved, which is `prime`?** A sentence answering that would inform the glossary
more than another broad taxonomy. It does not need to block the other inquiries.

No broader pass, implementation sequence, or new epic is proposed as already
authorized. The test of this assessment is whether it helps that conversation,
not whether it produces more work.
