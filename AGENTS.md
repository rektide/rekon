## Rekon working vocabulary

Terms for the shared sense-map, drawing on the earlier naming and constitutional
work. Definitions are working usage; disputed naming grammar remains with its
existing discussion, not settled by this glossary.

<section id="glossary-names-homes-and-relations">

### Names, homes, and relations

<dl>
<dt><dfn id="alias">Alias</dfn></dt>
<dd>
An alternate reference with a recoverable referent and scope;
abbreviations can be useful across different kinds of records.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="hat">Hat</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A concern-specific alias by which an actor can be addressed.
<em>Do not conflate with:</em> a new independent actor or a historical responsibility.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="role">Role</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A scoped responsibility an actor has in some work or relationship.
A role can explain a hat without being the actor's identity.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="bidirectional-link">Bidirectional link (⇄)</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A link marked to show that its far side links
back and that the pair is deliberately maintained together. The glyph is
recorded convention — a visible maintenance duty for readers and search, not
an enforcement mechanism. First adopted for the fronts thread:
<a href="/praxis/fronts/README.md#fronts-thread">fronts and horizons</a>.
<em>Do not conflate with:</em> a mere backlink, or acceptance of the linked work.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="channel">Channel</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A named selection of a current artifact, distinct from each
artifact it has selected over time.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="revision">Revision binding</dfn></dt>
<dd>
An immutable coordinate identifying the exact version
used for a claim or observation, rather than a route to whatever is current.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="sense-map">Sense-map</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A working arrangement of concepts and their relationships that
helps someone understand where they are and what a next action means.
</dd>
</dl>

</section>

<section id="glossary-knowledge-work-and-learning">

### Knowledge, work, and learning

<dl>
<dt><dfn id="magnitude">Magnitude</dfn></dt>
<dd>
An informal measure of how strongly an idea shapes and supports its project,
top to bottom. High-magnitude ideas can be axes for architecture, gating or
vocabulary; mid-magnitude ideas can be rich, valuable subsystems without
restructuring the whole project. Human calibration: <strong>8</strong> for
<a href="file:///home/rektide/src/bolderfs/design/access/equivalence-ungate0.glm53m.md">bolderfs equivalence-ungate</a>,
an unbuilt idea to leave retained readers ungated across a view swap when their
reachable bytes are proven identical, while controlling admission for new
handles and readers of changed bytes; <strong>6 for now</strong> for
<a href="file:///home/rektide/src/watchment/README.md">Watchment</a>, a rich
filesystem observation and state service. These are contextual judgments, not
fixed grades. <em>Do not conflate with:</em> priority, urgency, effort size or
acceptance.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="effort">Effort</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A continuing pursuit that may span actions, people, sessions,
repositories, and work items without making those things one identity.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="intent-front">Intent front</dfn></dt>
<dd>
An active line along which an effort's desired outcomes,
obligations, or ownership are being developed or reconsidered.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="opportunity-front">Opportunity front</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A coherent possible direction of work with a
recognizable payoff. It may become an active intent front, but naming it is
not selection, a ticket, or implementation authorization. Practice:
<a href="/praxis/fronts/README.md">fronts and horizons</a>.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="payoff-horizon">Payoff horizon</dfn></dt>
<dd>
The adoption, composition, evidence, or commitment
separating work from its useful return—not a calendar estimate. A tactical
move can have both immediate and strategic yield. Practice:
<a href="/praxis/fronts/README.md#distinctions">fronts and horizons</a>.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="work-anchor">Work anchor</dfn></dt>
<dd>
The native work item holding an undertaking's scope,
lifecycle, decisions, and links to the records through which it proceeds.
<em>Do not conflate with:</em> every document explaining the undertaking.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="claim">Claim</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A statement whose meaning, scope, grounds, and authority can be
examined; observation, interpretation, and obligation are different claims.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="evidence">Evidence</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A source's role in supporting, challenging, or limiting a claim
under stated conditions, not an intrinsic grade awarded to an artifact.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="provenance">Provenance</dfn></dt>
<dd>
The account of where a record or claim came from and how its
inputs, actors, actions, and revisions are related.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="case-story">Case story</dfn></dt>
<dd>
An account relating expected help, commissioned action,
actual conduct and results, and what can be learned from their alignment or gap.
</dd>
</dl>

</section>

<section id="glossary-action-and-delivery">

### Action and delivery

<dl>
<dt><dfn id="action">Action</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A bounded undertaking intended to change a situation or equip a
decision; an actual attempt is distinct from its reusable method.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="practice">Practice</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A repeatable way of joining purpose, procedure, evidence, and
reflection around a kind of action.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="flight">Flight</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A reusable coordination model for a series of agent promises, with explicit
spawn and join points expressing dependencies between work and decisions.
An actual flight instantiates the model with its own participants and outcomes.
<em>Do not conflate with:</em> a scheduler, a guarantee of success, or an
authorization to delegate. Practice: <a href="/praxis/flights/README.md">flights</a>.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="capability">Capability</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A repeatable job or discipline available for use in a
recognizable situation.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="skill">Skill</dfn></dt>
<dd>
An activation-bound interface through which a person or agent reaches
a capability and its relevant knowledge.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="exposure">Exposure</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A way a body of knowledge is presented or delivered to a reader
without necessarily creating another owner of its meaning.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="ambient">Ambient context</dfn></dt>
<dd>
Knowledge supplied before a task-specific need has caused
a reader to select deeper material.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="global-contribution">Global contribution</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A domain-owned proposal for what should be present in
shared ambient context, including the terms needed to interpret it.
<em>Do not conflate with:</em> global authority or proof that the contribution is loaded.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="globbing">Globbing</dfn></dt>
<dd>
Pattern-driven expansion of an assembly walk: a manifest entry containing glob
metacharacters directs the walk to find and select matching files,
deterministically sorted, each match still owning its own fragment metadata.
The pattern is the declaration — selection stays deliberate, loud on stale
directives, and checkable against published output.
<em>Do not conflate with:</em> shell pathname expansion, or discovery that
changes ambient content without a declaration.
Practice: <a href="/tool/assembly/README.md#globbing">enriched assembly</a>.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="context-pointer">Context pointer</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A reference that explains when and why its deeper material
should enter attention.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="bookmark">Bookmark</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A durable, owner-native mark on session material worth returning
to: a resolvable session target, a note saying why it exists, and free-form
tags saying what kind of mark it is. Practice and engine:
<a href="/praxis/bookmarking/README.md">bookmarking</a>.
<em>Do not conflate with:</em> the session itself, or acceptance of the marked work.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="bookmark-tag">Bookmark tag</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A free-form, case-sensitive label on a bookmark. Seeds such
as <code>crossroads</code> or <code>review</code> are recorded convention, not enforced vocabulary;
the live inventory is cotail's tag listing. Seed meanings:
<a href="/praxis/bookmarking/README.md">bookmarking's glossary</a>.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="crossroads">Crossroads</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A decision-dense junction in an effort — verified ground, open
paths, and the paths that lead back — marked while the reasoning is still
inspectable. Anchor example: <a href="file:///home/rektide/src/ocyc/design/fleet/crossroads0.glm53max.md">ocyc's fleet junction</a>.
Home: <a href="/praxis/bookmarking/README.md">bookmarking</a>.
For equipping the choice at that junction, see
<a href="/praxis/fronts/README.md#crossroads-relation">fronts and horizons</a>: strategic
options and tactical moves, compared across different payoff horizons.
</dd>
</dl>

</section>

### Honing and commitment

<dl>
<dt><dfn id="honing">Honing</dfn></dt>
<dd>
Deliberate revision through reading, comparison, and use while the
shape of a practice or exposure remains open.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="checkpoint">Checkpoint</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A preserved state against which a later revision or judgment
can be compared; preservation is not acceptance.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="acceptance">Acceptance</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A scoped human or owner decision selecting a direction or
result, distinct from its implementation or factual verification.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="promotion">Promotion</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A deliberate change in what standing or audience an artifact is
offered for, rather than merely moving or copying it.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="activation">Activation</dfn></dt>
<dd>
The event or condition in which a capability or guidance enters
actual use; unaccepted material can nevertheless be active.
</dd>

<dt><dfn id="verification">Verification</dfn></dt>
<dd>
A check of a stated property against named evidence and
conditions, not a synonym for acceptance or universal correctness.
</dd>
</dl>

<section id="doc-constitution-use-the-sense-map">

### Use the sense-map

Make technical work cumulative: recover intent, choose the action that helps,
inspect the evidence, preserve the learning, and let it inform the next course.
[Digest](/digest/README.md) finds present state and intent fronts;
[assess](/assess/README.md) judges fit and confidence;
[remake](/remake/README.md) considers a defensible landing sequence.
These are cooperating practices, not a compulsory pipeline.

Keep names and decisions with their native owners. Cite current homes for
navigation and revision-bound artifacts for exact evidence. A channel selecting
an accepted artifact is not that artifact. Explain cross-references; do not
assume similar words identify the same thing. Moving a durable source changes
its knowledge names: assess incoming references and agree the repair deliberately.

Domain modules maintain their README bodies; the existing SKILL symlinks load
those bodies. GLOBAL is the local proposal for shared ambient context. While
we hone, repetition across these exposures and the root view is intentional.
Keep the owner and any disagreement visible; do not strip useful definitions
merely to make a context count smaller. A tiny local glossary belongs before
instructions that rely on its terms; it need not repeat the entire shared glossary.

In Rekon, maintained explanations live with their domain owners. Shortcodes
(`con`, `prax`, `rkt`, the agent codes, and friends from the
[codes glossary](/codes/GLOSSARY.md)) are reference vocabulary, not directories.
The shared `tool/`
toolkit has top-level executable entrypoints and domain-grouped source/docs.
Use the [structure map](/constitution/structure.md) and
[short codes](/constitution/naming/README.md#short-codes); new Rekon topics do
not enter the retiring `doc/` bucket. In other projects, `design/<topic>/`
holds inquiry and `doc/<topic>/` is the default maintained home under the
pilot, subject to native conventions. Existing inquiry is not bulk-moved.
In upstream-owned repositories, fit upstream conventions for upstream work;
keep workspace-specific knowledge in a coherent carryable domain subtree.
Promotion synthesizes understanding rather than deleting the evidence it used.

When the practice itself is being tried, record the expected help and actual
result, including misalignment. [Case stories](/evidence/README.md#evidence-case-stories)
feed improvement; artifact completion alone does not establish usefulness.

Load the [constitution body](/constitution/README.md) for knowledge ownership,
waves, evidence, and promotion; load its [reading map](/constitution/index.md)
for prior rulings and unresolved alternatives. `AGENTS.md` is generated from
the root manifest; edit its domain fragments, then regenerate. The preserved
`AGENTS.old.md` is migration evidence, not the current producer. Check both
[assembly freshness and operating coverage](/tool/assembly/coverage.md).

## Writing, independent waves, and document connections

<section id="documentation-practice-durable-writing">

### Durable writing

- **ChatGPT/OpenAI agents:** make emitted thinking traces substantive, not just
  routine narration. Where relevant, surface alternatives actually considered,
  reasons and tradeoffs, assumptions, your lean and uncertainty, and what would
  change it. These are useful possibilities, not a mandatory checklist; scale
  depth to the work. This request concerns thinking traces, not a requirement
  to put them in ordinary messages. More:
  [candidates practice](/praxis/candidates/README.md#make-the-lean-inspectable).
- Use structured Markdown with descriptive links to canonical files and sections.
  Prefer bundle-root-relative links inside Rekon and canonical repository URLs
  for external sources; identify the repository when context does not already.
- Give knowledge documents honest OKF-style frontmatter: type, title,
  description, resource, status, author/model and date, sources actually read,
  and verification/review information only when justified. See
  [source practice](/refs/sources.md) and [the constitution](/constitution/README.md).
- **Include your current native session ID in OKF `sources:` whenever you create
  or substantively revise a knowledge document.** Use
  `resource: "opencode://<actual-session-id>"` with a title explaining the
  session's contribution. Use the ID supplied by the harness, not a petname,
  placeholder, or an example's ID. Preserve earlier session sources, including
  legacy URN and named-route forms. Keep your session ID stable and update its
  title as the contribution develops. For message/part citations, selected-source
  resolution, and portable exports, follow the
  [OpenCode URI convention](/refs/sources.md#our-opencode-session-convention).
- `index.md` is a grouped progressive-disclosure listing, not another concept
  document. `log.md` is dated update history, newest first. An addendum can retain
  an earlier argument while recording new understanding; mutable READMEs may
  integrate accepted conclusions directly.

</section>

<section id="documentation-practice-waves">

### Waves

- Waves are independent research/design outputs, commonly under `design/<topic>/`,
  not compulsory for every task. Start with the situation, present ask, inquiry
  directions, and what changed since previous work.
- Every wave filename ends in the current model's concise suffix. Use a numerical
  round starting at 0, e.g. `draft0.glm53m.md`; a main document is `<topic>.<model>.md`, a revision
  `<topic>2.<model>.md`, and a synthesis `draft0-syn0.<model>.md`. Never use
  the harness name as the model or copy another model's suffix.
- Do not read other authors' same-wave files unless directed; previous waves are
  available. Prefer new files for new arguments, preserve independent drafts,
  and maintain a README/index as the corpus becomes useful. Synthesize common
  themes and tensions, then compare strengths, weaknesses, and distinctive ideas.
- An accepted tip may have a suffix-less symlink; preserve the model-suffixed
  evidence. Acceptance is not deletion or bulk move; promotion synthesizes into
  the maintained home. For the fuller practice, read
  [the constitution's history guidance](/constitution/README.md#doc-constitution-history).

</section>

<section id="documentation-practice-doc-pass">

### Doc-pass

After substantial writing, search indexes and the surrounding corpus by concept,
not just exact keywords. Read candidate sources enough to distinguish meaningful
relationships. Add explained Cross-references (evidence, tension, precedent,
extension), useful reciprocal links, and index updates. Verify the links and
surrounding text. A separate search agent is optional when authorized; integrate
only findings checked against sources.

Precedent: [legacy writing practice](/AGENTS.old.md#documentation--reference-materials).

</section>
</section>

## Collaboration and architectural stewardship

<section id="collaboration-dispatch-and-return">

### Dispatch and return

- Run delegated agents in the background. Favor deliberate integration once
  prerequisite results are available; do not rush dependent documents.
- Aim to keep delegated sessions under **~300k context tokens**. Modest overshoot
  to 330–350k is acceptable near a coherent finish; let an almost-finished slice
  wrap rather than interrupting solely for this target. Start later stages in
  fresh sessions with concise handoffs and links to receipts and relevant code.
- Unless the user directs model selection, do not obey skills' demands for
  unavailable models. `general` is a capable default; for high-value work and
  deep research, ask which agents the user wants for which tasks. See
  [the general-agent guidance](/agents/general.md).
- When a user explicitly requests a model or variant, consult the
  [model lookup](/agents/models/README.md#variant-table) for override bases,
  variant aliases and availability; recheck uncertain or changed availability.
  Expand aliases to full variant names in tool calls (e.g. `#xh` → `#xhigh`);
  only set a model override on an explicit request. Agent definitions and the
  harness supply agent identity and configured defaults.
- When an agent hits a rate limit or quota exhaustion, use its stated fallback
  for the task; try the original agent again on each subsequent dispatch so it
  resumes when quota returns. Fallback is not a permanent switch.
- Prime agents with existing research and relevant Markdown. Ask them to return
  file/section references and to write and commit their own model-suffixed
  documents under the effort's domain, often `design/<topic>/`.
- Preserve other authors' files; favor new files over overwriting independent
  drafts. Use the continuation identifier actually returned by the harness
  (`sessionID` here) to resume the same agent.

</section>

<section id="collaboration-stewardship">

### Stewardship

- Delegate implementation and routine verification, not architectural
  responsibility. The parent assesses interfaces, domain concepts, reuse,
  tradeoffs, user intent, and verified upstream capabilities.
- Review at consequential architectural checkpoints rather than arbitrary
  file-by-file phases. Ask what became possible or impossible, choices and
  evidence, unresolved tensions, and the proposed next coherent slice.
- Trust reported checks; do not rerun them just to confirm. Read relevant code
  and evidence, and add verification when it answers a specific concern.
- Cooperatively anneal broader solutions. Explore plausible futures and surface
  their assumptions, costs, and opportunities. Distinguish possibilities worth
  preserving from capabilities authorized for implementation; let agents choose
  coherent slices within the agreed direction. Discuss forks that materially
  alter the shape rather than silently building or dismissing them.

</section>

<section id="collaboration-discussion">

### Discussion

Prefer multiple-choice questions allowing multiple selections when several
directions may matter. Explain the situation before the questions.

[assessment](/assess/README.md) supplies deeper review practices.

## Code — stage and change discipline

Selected for production assembly. The README/SKILL body owns the fuller practice;
these rules must be usable before an agent chooses deeper reading.

<section id="code-terms-in-use">

### Terms in use

- **Stage / checkpoint:** a coherent state of work / its preserved reference.
  Drafts, prompts, and research have stages too; saving one is not acceptance.
- **Correction:** a response to feedback, with a reason and an intended delta.
- **Scope:** the owned work a change may touch, not everything visible nearby.
- **Hunk:** the selection unit when logical changes share a file.

For the common [acceptance, promotion, and activation distinctions](/CONTEXT.md#acceptance),
use the shared vocabulary; this fragment adds only the terms its instructions need.

</section>

<section id="code-operating-weight">

### Operating weight

- commit each coherent stage when available, including prompts, research, drafts, and guidance: `jj commit -m "<description>" <files...>`. do not wait for the task or wave to end. before a review-driven revamp, save the present owned state; commit the revision separately and explain its delta and destination. checkpoint is not acceptance. the empty working-copy commit is expected; do not verify, move on. depth: /code/commits.md#commits-checkpoints
- changes intermingled **within** a file → `jj-hunk`: full `hunk-<sha256>` IDs only, always dry-run the spec first. load /code/jj-hunk.md before hunk-level work.
- review comments: discuss direction as well as committing the predecessor before editing; land each correction as its own well-argued commit. /code/review-handling.md
- plans carry no time estimates, ever. /code/planning.md
- scaffold repos with domain-grouped top-level dirs — never a flat dump, never
  `<pkg>-` prefixes repeated in the tree (`crates/parser`, not
  `crates/<pkg>-parser`); a registry-name prefix belongs to the package name,
  not the directory. when offering layout options, offer multiple plausible
  domain groupings. depth: /code/layout.md
- Mermaid diagrams should use meaningful node identifiers. Breaking changes
  are fine below 1.0; assess actual persisted-data or consumer obligations
  rather than inventing them.
- Preferred pre-alpha versioning default (optional; respect native policy): bump
  the minor for every public-interface addition/change/removal; internal-only
  fixes need no bump. Coordinated packages share a version and exact internal
  pins; update affected receiving locks too. Consumers adopt reviewed revisions;
  version equality is no compatibility promise. [Practice](/code/versioning.md).
- Let It Fail when appropriate: do not catch an error merely to print it; retain
  its stack and cause. Handle errors where recovery or a real boundary needs it.
- Do not add automatic prune/reconciliation behavior unless explicitly requested.
  Create/update declared state and leave cleanup deliberate.
- module entry point: /code/README.md

## jj and git operating rules

- Never use `jj edit`. Never squash unless explicitly instructed; humans review
  changes before squash. Do not push unless explicitly directed by the operator.
- Commit explicit owned paths with `jj commit -m "<description>" <files...>`;
  unrelated changes remain in the working copy. Inspect unfamiliar starting
  changes; after that assume sole ownership unless told otherwise. No routine
  `jj status` before every commit. The new empty working-copy commit is expected.
- Titles describe the work, not ticket IDs or temporary "phases". Put relevant
  ticket IDs in the commit message body. Use `jj diff --git` or `jj diff --stat`;
  plain diff is misleading without color.
- jj has its own DSL: consult `jj help -k revsets` and `jj help -k templates`
  before composing `-r` / `-T`. Ancestors `::@`, descendants `@::`, range `x..y`;
  no `::~`. Fields are bare (`change_id.short()`), not `commit.change_id`;
  concatenate strings with `++`.
- Name dated bookmarks `<feature>-<YYYYMMDD>` using the local date the work was
  last active (recover with `jj op log`), not automatically today's date.
- Working-copy author dates come from creation and can become stale. Read
  [date surgery](/vcs/jj/rewrites.md#backdating-a-fresh-commit-stale-working-copy-dates)
  before deliberate correction through colocated git; jj itself cannot set them.
- For hunks, use full IDs and a dry-run as described in the code fragment.
  Wholesale rewrites do not hunk-split into clean intermediates: preserve the
  rewritten version, restore the parent version, apply/commit the minimal change,
  then restore/commit the full rewrite. Load [jj-hunk](/code/jj-hunk.md).
- [The jj guide](/vcs/jj/README.md) owns command recipes and forensics.

<section id="jj-jj-waltz-workspaces">

### jj-waltz workspaces

`jw` owns workspace lifecycle; `jj` owns graph/fetch/bookmarks. [Usage guide](/vcs/jj/waltz.md):

```sh
eval "$(jw shell init zsh)"  # enables cd; add creates, switch creates/switches
jw add worker-a worker-b --at 'dev@origin' --cow --no-colocate
jw s worker-a               # ^ default, - previous; list/status/doctor inspect
jw reseed worker-a worker-b --from /path/to/prepared-source --source-at 'dev@origin' \
  --copy-ignored node_modules --at 'dev@origin' --dry-run
jw reseed --group workers --at 'dev@origin' --dry-run # optional local config
```

Reseed keeps roots; omit `--at` to keep feature revisions. Remove `--dry-run` to
refill; pause writers. Prepare source with JJ/install first. [Groups](/vcs/jj/waltz.md#jj-waltz-groups)
are optional literal-name lists in default-workspace `.jwreseed.local.toml`;
named use needs none. `--copy-ignored` replaces selected roots with private CoW
copies; links share symlinks. `--replace-content` deletes other unrecorded contents
(including `.env`) except `.jj`. Pending refill: `jw reseed NAME --resume` before
snapshotting. [Preparation, restrictions, recovery](/vcs/jj/waltz.md#jj-waltz-source).

## JavaScript and TypeScript

- Prefer TypeScript. Use explicit `.ts` extensions for local imports and the
  `node:` prefix for Node builtins. Keep class members public; visibility does
  not guarantee API stability.
- Prefer pnpm. Add dependencies through `pnpm install <package>@latest` (or `-D`),
  rather than hand-editing dependency entries. Run source directly with
  `node path/to/cli.ts` in development and examples; build output is for npm
  distribution only.
- Prefer promise-based async APIs and `node:fs/promises`, not sync methods or
  callbacks where promises exist. Start independent work together; await when
  its value is needed.
- Default tools: oxfmt formatting, oxlint linting, tsdown distribution, vitest
  tests when no test stack exists, and `@typescript/native-preview` / `tsgo`
  for typechecking. Bundling does not typecheck. With TypeScript configuration,
  use noEmit, allowImportingTsExtensions, and isolatedDeclarations where suitable.
- For complex CLI use gunshi with `@gunshi/plugin-completion`; for config use
  c12 or at least xdg-basedir. Respect a project's established tooling; the
  accepted Rekon toolchain is Rust-first for new capabilities.
- Compose `build:*`, `check:*`, and `fix:*` tasks using concurrently glob targets,
  such as `concurrently 'pnpm:check:*'`. Include a dev/watch target. Read
  [the JS guide](/code/js/README.md) for vite-plus version/peer setup and full recipes.
- Executable modules should detect direct invocation, asynchronously realpath
  `process.argv[1]` before comparing it, then dynamically load their main module.
  Use `.then` so importing the module does not block on invocation detection;
  include a shebang and executable bit where appropriate.
- Web components use wc-toolkit (Web Components Toolkit) with descriptive JSDoc
  to generate Custom Element Manifests; wire manifest generation into the toolkit.
</section>

## Beads — readable work IDs and deliberate tracking

- Ticketing is user-shaped; use existing work items and ask when tracking scope
  is unclear. When using beads, work from the project directory, not `bd -C`.
- Check `bd config get issue_prefix`. Always pass a stable, human-readable
  `--id`; never use autogenerated IDs. Namespace child IDs under their epic:
  `<prefix>-<effort>-<capability>`. Add the parent-child edge explicitly too.
- Epics explain why and how the system fits together; children state concrete
  implementation scope and acceptance criteria. Search before creating duplicates.
- `bd dep add A B --type blocks` means A depends on B. Dependent first;
  prerequisite second. For `--type parent-child`, child first, parent second.
- Preserve history with `bd update --append-notes`; close completed work with a
  reason stating what was completed and verified. Prefer `bd rename` to duplicate
  replacement tickets when the undertaking is the same.

```sh
bd list --status open --desc-contains "<topic>" --sort priority --limit 20
bd show <issue-id>
bd create --id <prefix>-<short-name> --title "<outcome>" --type task --priority P2
bd update <issue-id> --claim --append-notes "<findings and next step>"
bd dep add <child-id> <epic-id> --type parent-child
bd dep add <dependent-id> <prerequisite-id> --type blocks
bd close <issue-id> --reason "<completion and verification>"
```

- Dolt is the source of truth. Keep `export.auto=true` where supported; use
  `bd export -o .beads/issues.jsonl` when a tracked snapshot is needed and automatic
  export is unavailable. JSONL export is not the current backup/restore format.
- Never run `bd prime`; report which tool/instruction requested it. It generates
  unwanted `AGENTS.md` and `CLAUDE.md` guidance.
- Read [beads practice](/praxis/beads/README.md) for linked work, supersession,
  rename, and version-aware storage/recovery. Ticket pointer upkeep is below.

## Flights — spawn, join, and carry evidence forward

A **flight** composes agent work as explicit promises: bounded spawns, joins
that name what a dependent step needs, and evidence carried forward. It is a
coordination model, not a scheduler and not delegation authority; humans own
authorization and decisions. Practice: [flights](/praxis/flights/README.md).

The ready reusable model is **random-combo**: draw N weighted options per game
(three by default) from a human-supplied pool, with persistent winner decay,
then join independent game responses before comparison. Its generator runs
without install from the Rekon checkout:

```sh
node tool/random-combo.ts --rows 4 --slots 3 --seed example FILE   # --help for more
```

Flight model: [random-combo](/praxis/flights/random-combo.md); generator
contract: [random-combo tool](/tool/random-combo/README.md). Depth pair for
flight design: [parallax](/praxis/parallax/README.md) (why independent views
differ) and [evidence gates](/assess/gates/README.md) (arrival vs readiness).

## Coverage — account for threads and omissions

When the human asks **“coverage?”**, gather the relevant prompt or conversation's
threads, re-present their dispositions, and name important adjacent topics not dealt
with in this pass. Offer an unasked-for coverage check when it would help, including
semi-periodic check-ins during longer work; use judgment about context and frequency,
not every turn. State the examined scope and uncertainty; distinguish suggestions
from decisions and results. Do not turn threads into tickets or commitments. Deeper
practice: [coverage](/praxis/coverage/README.md).

For user-facing thread accounts and other reports, use these presentation cues
when helpful, as hashtags or ordinary language:

| Cue | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `#background` | Existing context or current ground. |
| `#iterative` | Extension or revision of existing work; not necessarily easy. |
| `#net-new` | A capability or approach new to the stated scope. |
| `#forward-looking` | A possibility or direction beyond established ground. |

These lenses can combine: future-looking work can extend existing mechanisms,
and net-new implementation can be ready now. They do not establish proposed,
recommended, accepted, commissioned, implemented, or verified standing; say
that separately when relevant. A recommendation tag is not authorization.
They are not ticket or bookmark tags, runtime classification, or mandatory on
every answer. [Coverage presentation practice](/praxis/coverage/README.md#present-the-nature-of-a-thread)
connects them to digest, fronts, and an inspectable lean.
</section>

## Bookmarking — mark session material worth returning to

Mark decision-dense junctions (crossroads), explicit summaries, and parked
review/design gates while the reasoning is still inspectable. Write the note as
the evidentiary sentence — why this mark exists and what it bears on — and give
it free-form tags. A bookmark preserves an inspectable state; it decides
nothing. Practice and seed glossary:
[bookmarking](/praxis/bookmarking/README.md).

The engine is cotail's landed CLI:

```sh
cotail bookmark create -s ses_... --tag crossroads --note "why this junction matters"
cotail bookmark list --tag review --tag-match any   # top-down review view
cotail bookmark tags                                # live tag inventory
```

Tags are convention, not enforced vocabulary; the live inventory is
`cotail bookmark tags`. Until cotail's session-env ticket lands, pass `-s`
explicitly.

## Tools — check knowledge documents

**`rkt` — quick recipes from any checkout.** Verify Markdown you just wrote,
or check generated output, in seconds:

```sh
R="cargo run --quiet --manifest-path ~/src/rekon/tool/rust/Cargo.toml"
$R check FILE...                      # links, anchors, fences, whitespace
$R check --okf DOC.md                 # + frontmatter and session provenance
$R combine --check                    # AGENTS.md fresh?
$R codes --check                      # codes glossary fresh?
$R toc FILE --check                   # nav region fresh?
$R audit-context                      # declared coverage of the bundle
```

Checks are read-only; `--check` never writes. Use `rkt` for Markdown
maintenance in any repo, not just rekon's.

- After writing or revising Markdown, check the explicit owned files with
  `check FILE...` (from any checkout via the `$R` alias above). It verifies
  local link/image targets, heading/explicit-ID anchors, fenced-code closure
  and trailing whitespace outside code, without editing files.
- Add `--okf` for knowledge documents: require the selected frontmatter fields,
  a native OpenCode session source and resolvable local `sources[].resource` paths.
  Ordinary Markdown does not need this profile. The command recognizes session
  authorities; the author must still supply the actual contributing session.
- `/...` links resolve at the nearest `.jj`/`.git` ancestor of each checked file;
  use `--root DIR` to select another bundle root. `file:///...` links retain their
  absolute checkout meaning. Remote URLs and semantic claims are not verified.
- For generated `AGENTS.md`, edit its source fragments and run `combine`, then
  `combine --check`; document checking does not establish assembly freshness.
  Use [the checker guide](/tool/check/README.md) for parser/profile limits and
  [the toolkit map](/tool/README.md) for ToC, scoped OKF extraction, the codes
  glossary, and coverage tools.
